How I Got Here

Software engineer, tech lead, strategy consultant, product manager — each role built on the last.

I started as a full-stack developer at Cognizant, writing code for S&P Global and MetLife. By my third year, I was leading a team — owning architecture decisions, mentoring junior devs, and learning that the hardest problems weren't technical, they were about alignment. That's what pulled me toward product.

An MBA at IIM Kozhikode and a strategy consulting stint at Deloitte gave me the business lens. Four years of advising Fortune 500s on digital transformation taught me how large organizations actually make decisions, how to navigate ambiguity, and how to tie product work to business outcomes.

I brought both worlds together at Ford — and was promoted to Product Line Manager within two years, one of the youngest in the organization. Today I own a 4-product analytics portfolio supporting over $5B in annual service and parts revenue. The engineering background means I can go deep with my teams. The consulting background means I know how to connect what we build to what the business needs.

Things I've Built

Active at Ford

AI-Powered Resource Intelligence Platform

The existing performance review process at Ford was broken in a few specific ways: feedback was generic and only written during year-end reviews, 360-degree feedback collection had poor response rates and no centralized tracking, and managers walked into 1:1 meetings with no data to anchor the conversation. Reviews felt like a formality instead of a growth tool.

What I built

  • A single platform where managers can request, track, and collect 360-degree feedback from a reportee's peers — all in one place, with automated reminders.
  • AI-generated consolidated feedback summaries written in Ford's standard review format, so managers don't start from a blank page.
  • Dynamic performance metrics pulled directly from Jira and GitHub — giving managers real data on delivery velocity, code contributions, and sprint performance.
  • Objective tracking that lets managers see whether reportees are on track against their annual goals, updated in real-time.
Saves ~15–20 hours per manager per review cycle
Active at Ford

Product Management Operating System

When I joined Ford, I noticed that PM practices varied wildly across teams. There was no shared playbook, no consistent way to track metrics, and no regular forum for PMs to share learnings or challenge each other's thinking. I set out to fix that.

What I established

  • Quarterly Product Line Reviews — structured reviews of each product's health, roadmap progress, and commercial impact with leadership visibility.
  • Quarterly PM Newsletter — a digest of product wins, learnings, and best practices circulated across the PM organization.
  • Jira Best Practices Playbook — standardized how teams use Jira for backlog management, sprint planning, and reporting so metrics are actually comparable across products.
  • Monthly AI Idea Sessions — a recurring forum where PMs and engineers pitch AI use cases for their product lines, with the best ideas getting fast-tracked to prototyping.
  • Metrics Tracking Framework — defined consistent KPIs across the portfolio so leadership can compare product health apples-to-apples.
Adopted across Ford's Service & Parts analytics organization
